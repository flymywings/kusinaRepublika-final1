# The Mayor and the Woodpecker

Original fiction set in a French village.

The narration runs 92 words. At 3.5 words per second it takes about 26 seconds before pauses. Aim for roughly 30 seconds with a brief beat before “Wood” and room for the final line.

## Narration

This mayor offered a bag of gold for one dead woodpecker.

He’d taxed his village in France for a solid-gold statue of himself.

Then the bird pecked its head.

He doubled the reward and sent his guards.

They chased it around the square. Their shouting brought everyone outside.

The bird flew right back and knocked a chip off the statue.

A carpenter turned it over.

Wood.

The mayor had paid for wood and paint. The reward was their gold.

By sunset, the village had its gold back. The woodpecker kept the statue.

## Story direction

One male narrator, conversational and dryly amused. Begin with the absurdly specific reward, hold back the statue's material, and let the pursuit bring witnesses to the fraud. Show the chip's wood grain before the narrator connects the reward to the stolen tax money. At sunset, the village recovers its gold, the mayor leaves his chain of office behind, and the bird keeps the statue.

The opening is a reward being offered for the bird, already in progress. Subsequent beats establish the apparently valuable statue, escalate the mayor's effort to stop the pecking, gather a crowd through that commotion, reveal the chip's unpainted underside, and return to the statue as the bird's home. Reuse the opening reward bag as the visible money the mayor has retained.

Warm setup; cooler reveal. Use restrained action coverage and selective motivated reframes under the V5 camera guidance. Bigger reactions belong to the public discovery. Keep the carpenter's inspection, chip, faces, and white captions readable. A short quiet beat before “Wood” supplies emphasis; retain atmosphere underneath it.

## Edit plan

1. **Reward and statue.** Open at counter height as the mayor drops a gold pouch beside a sketch of the woodpecker. Move past the pouch to establish the statue behind him. Use a low three-quarter view of the pedestal to make the statue feel imposing. The pouch and bird sketch make the bounty readable in the first two seconds. Establish the mayor's chain of office and show a villager putting a tax coin into the same marked pouch during the setup.
2. **Chase.** Show the mayor adding coins to the marked pouch as he doubles the reward and sends his guards. Track the bird sideways at its height. Let a market awning pass close to camera, with the guards in the square and the village roofs farther back. Show the pedestal and the guards' route in the same view before following the bird around it. Settle when the bird lands. Bring villagers out of the bakery, shop, and upstairs windows in response to the commotion. Their attention follows the guards and then shifts to the bird.
3. **Chip reveal.** Follow the chip down with its painted face visible. Settle over the carpenter's shoulder as he turns it over. Fill the frame with enough of the chip to read both the painted edge and exposed grain. Show the grain by “Wood” and hold through the word. Let the nearest villager look from chip to mayor, then show him tightening his grip on the marked pouch. Let cool shadows and a quieter score carry the turn while the hand and chip stay clear.
4. **Sunset callback.** Use a 6–8 frame crossfade at 30 fps into the sunset shot. Align the statue's head across both views. Blend the pictures beneath the captions and carry the square's ambience through the transition. Show the marked pouch back in a villager's hands and the mayor setting down his chain of office. End on the bird in its nest on “the statue.”

## Narration revision

The original skill and its Bridge example build from a paradox to escalating action, a visible cause, a consequence, and a callback. The draft uses that order over roughly 30 seconds. The reward rises once, the guards draw witnesses to the square, and the bird's return creates the evidence those witnesses see. The same pouch connects the opening offer to the stolen tax money and its return.

The 20 local reference transcripts informed four choices:

- **01, 04, 05, 08, 11, 17.** Open with the odd action and leave its purpose for later. The gold bounty sets up the question before the statue is introduced.
- **02, 03, 06, 10, 12, 13, 19.** Let the next action change the risk or test a claim. The mayor sends guards to suppress the problem and their chase gives it an audience.
- **07, 09, 16, 18, 20.** Make the evidence easy to recognize. The chip's grain settles the material question, then the marked pouch shows where the money went. Reference 18 offers a compact example of a short escalation followed by a single mechanism.
- **14, 15.** Bring the original object back after the outcome changes. The village reclaims its gold and the statue becomes the woodpecker's home.
