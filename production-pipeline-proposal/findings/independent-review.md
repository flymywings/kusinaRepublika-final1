## Verification and limits

- **Speech integrity.** The source hash matches edit-map.json. I decoded source and exported audio with `ffmpeg -i <file> -map 0:a:0 -ac 1 -ar 48000 -f f32le -n <temp.raw>`. For each retained word window, I compared the mono export with source at the sample offset round((at−from)×48000), searching ±1600 samples in two-sample increments and using normalized dot products on every 24th sample. All 66 windows peaked at a lag of 0 or 2 samples. Correlations ranged from 0.346 to 0.9995, with the lowest values in short final-word windows containing effects or silence. The 6 ms source windows on each side *inside* all four retained intervals have absolute peaks below 0.000113. The final “face” has signal through about source 35.05, 200 ms before the last cut. I found no evidence of a clipped, substituted or retimed word. The export is a limited, gained mix with AAC coding. The voice is multiplied by 6 ms edge ramps, so a literal claim of bit-identical samples at those boundaries would be incorrect.
- **Caption text and timing.** I normalized punctuation and case and compared all 123 source entries with the approved narration. Filtering by the edit map leaves 66 words across nine sentences. I extracted the burned 80%-height caption band on the first scheduled frame of every retained word and read all 66 in order. No substitution, missing word or horizontal crop appeared. At a 270 px picture width the declared font scales to 18 px. Dark outlines remained visible over the white blouse and box. The test above verifies alignment to the authored starts, while the acoustic comparison establishes the take’s placement. It does not establish phonetic onset to ±2 frames for every individual word.
- **Other cue checks.** The 7.52 door and 7.58 bell correspond to visual triggers at 7.50 and 7.58. Her 10.50 bow and the 16.68 offer correspond to rig triggers at 10.50 and 16.65. The opening fork passes in front of his face and is already off the face by its clink. A struck surface is outside the visible shot. The chair cues cannot be verified as physical contacts from the picture. Event times refer to sample placements in sound.mjs. I did not isolate the perceptual attack of every practical in the final mix.
- **Identity and continuity.** The blond hair, ear jewelry and apron, and the dark hair and blouse remain recognizable across the sampled frames. The exterior switches to the specified school outfit. The colored irises emerge on the scripted turn. I saw no unprogrammed face swap. The comparison baseline is a 540×960, 24 fps, 42.5 s video, sampled directly at four-second intervals for reference. Baseline framing and wardrobe were not treated as mandatory.
- **Output safety.** All three QA commands use pipeline/qa/common.mjs:75–97. I raced two calls to newOutput with different temporary text payloads and the same new destination. Exactly one fulfilled and its bytes survived. The current MP4 renderer also publishes with an exclusive hard link in the installed finalize-fast-start.js. The still-image writer differs as documented above. Stills batches publish one frame at a time, so an error later in a batch leaves a valid prefix and a straight retry hits the existing files.
- **Limits.** Inspection used a silent 4 fps pass, selected consecutive frames, burned caption strips, source code and decoded PCM. I did not listen to the mix, watch uninterrupted real-time playback, inspect every pixel transition in all 732 frames, or test platform UI overlays on a physical phone. No generalized flicker, clipping-free playback, or listening approval can be inferred. The delivered pilot hash matched QA.md. No project output was regenerated.
