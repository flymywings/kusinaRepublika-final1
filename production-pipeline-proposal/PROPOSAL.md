## 1. The pitch: "Same second, her side"

Show the moment she runs twice.

1. **His version.** From her seat, looking up at him. He fills the frame, backlit and cold, and his shadow climbs over the cake. A fork drops: *clink*. Chair scrape. Door bell. She's gone.
2. **Rewind.** The bell plays backwards and everything stops on the falling fork.
3. **Her version.** The same three sounds in a warm room. We look from his eyes to her: a skyline of plates, both cheeks packed, eyes huge, blush climbing to her hairline. She wasn't looking at a monster. She'd been caught.
4. **The turn.** Back to the first camera position in normal light. The monster is a tall boy in a shop, holding a tray.

The audience makes Rintaro's mistake with him, then gets corrected by a picture before the narration explains it.
It is funny at step 3 and tender at step 4. The repeated *clink* tells the ear it is the same second before the eye works it out.

It also suits the frontal-only rule. Each side is a point of view, so each character faces the lens. No profile or rear drawing is needed.

### The apology, through his reaction

- He inhales and starts to bow.
- She is already bowing, lower and faster, cake box held out at arm's length. He stops halfway down, his apology unused.
- Halfway down, a 190 cm boy is at eye level with a 148 cm girl. He has stopped looming, for a physical reason.
- "She's never found him scary." The music and room tone drop out. Hold on him: one blink, eyes to the box, brows release, shoulders drop. No smile yet.
- The smile waits for the final line and stays small.

## 7. Architecture

Remotion and Three.js stay. No new renderer and no agent framework.

```
pipeline/
  kit/v1/{look,cast,acting,camera,world,sound}/   frozen copies from accepted V8
  cli.mjs                                        ingest | align | preview | sheet | qa | compare | deliver
shorts/fragrant-flower/
  brief.md  bible.md  checkpoint.json
  script/  voice/  cast/  sets/  shots.ts  cues.ts
  out/<version>/{film.mp4,contact/,qa.json,qa.md}
```

Old project folders become read-only baselines. A version folder is never overwritten.

Each shot spec records: start and end word, location, subject, what changes, each character's want and pose keys, camera start and end with reason, focus and light, contacts, sound cues, and the handoff.

### Visual bible

- **Medium:** flat frontal 2D cartoon cards in inked, lit Three.js worlds, 9:16.
- **Cast:** bean heads, simple ink faces, fine limbs. Hair, costume and hardware carry identity.
- **Light:** warm reality. Cold light and exaggerated scale belong to Rintaro's fear only. This replaces the default warm-to-blue arc for this story, because here the realization is the warmth.
- **Depth:** a near, middle and far layer in every shot. No shot is a character on a blank wall.
- **Captions:** one to three words in a fixed band, digits for numbers, never over a mouth or key prop.

### Preservation

| Risk | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Face | Named expression keys interpolate geometry. No cross-dissolved faces, no off-palette eye fills |
| Anatomy and proportions | Height, head, neck and limb constants live in the rig. Shots cannot override them |
| Hair and silhouette | Regression render of every expression at sheet, medium and close scale |
| Ground | Distance-driven gait, world-space planted soles, a shadow per foot |
| Props | Named hand and prop anchors, one owner per prop per frame, handoff frames asserted |
| Framing | Projected silhouette and caption-band bounds on every frame |
| Close-ups | Card textures redrawn at delivery scale |

### Components

- **Rigs:** Rintaro, Kaoruko, a frontal customer variant.
- **Expressions:** guarded, reading, stricken, mid-apology, released, small smile. Attentive, caught, three-stage blush, sincere, matter-of-fact.
- **Moves:** recoil, bolt, bow with interrupt, offer, swallow, shoulder release.
- **Camera:** push to information, settle, reveal pan, matched point of view, return to setup, locked hold.
- **Café:** exterior and street, door and bell, display case with reflection, counter, booth, window, stocked background, two lighting states.

## 8. QA

**Automatic:** dimensions, fps, frame count and duration, full decode, missing assets, audio presence, loudness, peak and unexpected silence, black and frozen spans, transcript against script, caption timing and bounds, camera continuity, contacts, identity regression, cue-to-action offset within two frames.
Loudness target for this pilot: -15 LUFS, true peak no higher than -1.5 dBTP.

**Review of the exported file, every draft:**
1. Muted and unbriefed at 2 fps: what happens and where does the eye go?
2. Every transition, contact and expression change at 30 fps: anatomy, clipping, floating, framing.
3. With timed transcript, captions and audio envelope: does each sound and cue land on its action and word?
4. Against the named reference beat and the baseline: clarity, emotion, pacing, and what is still bland.

Limit: I inspect frames and audio signals. I cannot experience playback or hear a performance or mix. Each report says so, and your normal-speed watch with sound is the final check on feel.

**Repair loop:** record the issue with timecode and cause, then fix and rerender only that range with handles.
After two focused passes on the same issue, stop. Restage if that is routine. If it changes direction, bring you two clips and a recommendation. A later change reopens the checks it affects.

**Checkpoint after every stage:** inputs and hashes, approvals with your exact words, artifacts, findings, fixes, pass count per issue, interventions, next action. A new session resumes from that file.

## 9. Measuring autonomy

Each full Short has 20 fixed work units:
intake, facts, script, cast, wardrobe, voice ingest, alignment, storyboard, shot specs, environments, props, acting, gait and contact, camera, light, sound, captions, QA and repair, comparison, delivery.

- **Autonomy** = units completed without an unplanned intervention, divided by units attempted.
- **90–95%** = one or two units needing you on a full Short.
- An intervention is any correction, defect you found first, missing decision, or technical rescue.
- Planned gates (direction and words, new cast, acceptance, publishing) are counted separately, so they cannot hide in the score.
- Also report your active minutes, defects that escaped QA, repair passes and render time. Skipped units are not counted as successes.

The 24 s pilot attempts fewer units, so its score will be reported as provisional.
