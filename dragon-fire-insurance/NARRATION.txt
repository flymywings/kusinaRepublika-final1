A dragon sold fire insurance for one coin per house. Easy money. He’d just stop breathing fire. The villagers lined up. He insured the bakery, the forge, even a wooden inn. Then an ember burned down an empty shed. Its owner presented his policy. One coin in. A wheelbarrow of gold out. The dragon reread the promise. Any fire. Now he guarded ovens, chased sparks, and slept beside a bucket. Then a child lit one birthday candle. The dragon came charging across the square. The village was insured against fire. The cake wasn’t insured against buckets.
