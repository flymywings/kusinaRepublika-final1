# V2 hook direction and reference check

Status: proposed direction, researched on 2026-11-06. No production scene or camera changes have been made for this study. An opening preview and an unbriefed comprehension check remain required.

## Intent

Earn attention during the first two seconds, before the opening sentence has finished. The supplied recording's first sentence occupies approximately 0–4.56 seconds according to local recognition. Preserve its wording and the delayed planting reveal.

Start with active inspection of a rough diamond, not a passive gem beauty shot or a planting shot. The audience should immediately follow a person examining evidence and notice two men watching the outcome. The open claim setting must distinguish the scene from a jewelry-shop sale. Do not invent a mine shaft.

## Proposed staging

- Frames 0–18: an investor is already raising a rough stone toward his eyes. Hand, stone, and interested face are readable against his darker coat. The two sellers form separate silhouettes behind him; claim terrain remains visible.
- Frames 18–48: one symmetric backward/upward camera glide follows the rising hand and discloses the sellers and setting. Preserve the stone's screen position sufficiently to keep the eye oriented. Gain prop readability through camera framing, not changing its physical size.
- Frames 48–60: settle into investor/stone/seller-pair composition. Sellers watch the inspection. Do not pile on walking, payment, pointing, or a cutaway.
- Frames 60–137: a restrained push carries the rest of the sentence. A brief natural glint and approving reaction can support the real-diamond phrase, timed from final word alignment. Keep the method concealed. Hand off coherently into Colorado and the discovery sequence.

Warm key light, cool shadows, grounded figures, foreground/background depth, and restrained lens blur support the action. Keep hands and eyes clear of captions. Sound starts under the voice without a loud generic impact; cloth and handling accents belong to visible movements.

## Opening review gate

Review a moving first-five-second preview before broad polishing. Without explanation, a reviewer should identify the inspected object, the active observer, and who is watching him. With narration, the scene must communicate the claim's persuasive evidence without suggesting that individual stones are the product being sold. Check at phone size and at normal speed, then inspect frames where the eye loses the hand or object.

Reject floating gems, overlapping seller silhouettes, tiny faces, hands obscured by captions, excessive blur, camera motion that outruns the action, a jewelry-sale interpretation, premature planting gestures, or a weak handoff into the setting. A clean isolated preview is provisional; recheck it after integration with the final camera, captions, and sound. Research and reviewer agreement do not prove audience retention.
