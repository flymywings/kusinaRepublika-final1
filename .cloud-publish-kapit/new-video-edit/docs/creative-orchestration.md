## Quality requirements

### Lessons adopted after accepted V8 — 2026-11-18

- **Review the story before spending on voice.** Start with a clear contradiction, concrete developments and an earned emotional payoff. Trim repeated explanation. Continue evaluating the hook after approval; bring exact wording changes to the user before replacing approved narration.
- **Preserve identity through movement.** Use verified source artwork, one coherent outfit and the approved production cast. Simple faces remain the default. Reuse the V8 performance approach without copying its story-specific timing into a new film.
- **Animate the emotional transition.** A continuous camera cannot hide an instant face swap. Coordinate eyes, brows, mouth, hands and shoulders; let recognition read before moving attention. Preserve ink-and-white facial construction through intermediate states. Do not blend eye fills into unfamiliar colours or dissolve two faces over each other. Tie speaking activity to the actual phrase; close the mouth during quiet spans.
- **Direct the camera around attention and physical space.** Prefer connected takes with deliberate pushes, inserts and pullbacks. Clear the complete character/prop silhouette and the camera sight line, including doors, handles and foreground props. Inspect moving foot contact and clear paths, not merely actor centres. Do not add a default dark ending fade; keep the closing image visible.
- **Separate speech pace from breathing room.** Use approximately190–195 overall narration WPM as the current starting benchmark, then judge the actual take. Speed speech selectively while preserving that recording's pauses. Twenty pauses belong to this accepted take, not every future recording. Retiming requires remapping acting, captions and sound together.
- **Plan sound with the beats.** Assign location beds, practical actions and selective emotional accents before production. Make them audible in integrated previews, then finalize cues against locked picture timing. A populated effects list or correct loudness does not prove a full, balanced mix. Preserve quiet narration without making the world disappear.
- **Give captions their own display check.** Display explicit numerical quantities as digits, including compound ages such as45-year-old. Preserve spoken wording/timing separately. Check final rendered captions, not only the transcript.
- **Review the exported result and record acceptance.** Inspect dense reaction/transition sequences as well as whole-film coverage. Keep physical checks, creative review and listening claims distinct. The user supplies normal-speed audiovisual feedback and acceptance. Store the accepted version and exact file identity; do not assume the newest render is approved.

These changes are refinements of the existing script → production character sheet → voice → autonomous production → integrated-draft feedback workflow. They add no routine approval stages or new agent framework. Keep one lead, bounded useful workers, economical targeted renders and the existing revision stopping rule. User8/10 acceptance is the outcome recorded here; it is not audience retention data or proof of virality.

## Bounded iteration and accounting

Starting internal policy: after two focused repair passes on a persistently weak sequence, stop repeating local repairs and decide whether to restage, replace the execution or report a concrete blocker. This is a decision point, not a requirement to stop after two passes or permission for unlimited restarts.

Use targeted low-resolution renders while iterating and final-resolution exports after integrated review. Reopen affected ranges after later changes. Track model usage where available, render time, revision count, unresolved findings and user acceptance in the existing project ledger. Mark unavailable metrics unknown. No resource savings claim without measurements.

Respect explicit resource limits. If the standard remains unattainable within the available envelope, identify the blocker and show the best defensible result with an honest limitation. Never relabel failure as an approved-quality draft.
