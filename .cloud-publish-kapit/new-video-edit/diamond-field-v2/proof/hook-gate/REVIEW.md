# Opening gate — FIX

Reviewed actual 5.00s preview at 5 samples/s before reading narration, then references 07,09,14 at 2 samples/s and upstream motion report. This is narrative/camera judgment, not engagement measurement.

## Independent account before narration
A smiling blue-suited man raises a pale jewel toward the viewer. A brown hat sits behind/above his head; a pullback reveals a green-clothed person on the right and gems on disturbed ground. The pale jewel is the important object. Neither visible person's eyes clearly follow it or each other. No sale or exchange is enacted. First two seconds supply an object reveal and a verbal contradiction ('fake diamond mine'), but the visual question is weak: it reads as a man proudly showing a diamond.

## Required changes
- 0.00–4.60s: Separate the brown-hat seller laterally from the blue man's head silhouette. His face is occluded almost throughout; the opening reads as one man with two stacked hats, while narration says 'Two men'. Reveal both faces by 'Two men', keeping blue face and jewel dominant.
- 0.80–2.00s: Give the jewel an explicit recipient/reaction. Turn observer's gaze/head toward the raised stone, and angle seller toward observer rather than all three facing the viewer. A restrained lean/reaching hand or clear inspection pose can show the sale proposition without adding another explanatory beat.
- 1.60–4.40s: Pullback currently reveals space, then holds nearly the same tableau for about three seconds. Use the reveal to establish the mine/ground-gem relationship: settle with one foreground ground gem clearly larger/readable and eye/hand direction connecting to it. Keep a subtle deliberate push toward the object or reaction during the contradiction, rather than parking on three front-facing figures.
- 4.60–5.00s: The second pullback happens on 'Colorado' but exposes little new narrative information. Use its endpoint as the geographical establishing handoff, preserving seller/observer separation and the same gem-ground relationship; avoid a disconnected reset before the next shot.

## Readability/contact
Blue face and raised pale stone are readable at a 216x384 phone proxy; mouth and brow changes register. The held gem appears attached to the black hand and travels consistently with it. No obvious contact slip in the sampled movement. Ground stones are tiny at the wider endpoint; rubies on the right compete less than sky/large face but do not yet communicate a rich mine. No head or prop is accidentally clipped; leg crop in the first close-up is intentional. Rear seller occlusion is the decisive framing failure.

## Reference comparison
07 makes two cards readable, then camera and pointing isolate the relevant card. 09 cuts to the ticket at the scanning action and returns to a changed face. 14 establishes a buyer/seller relationship, then advances toward the contradictory document. Borrow the cause-and-attention principle, not the shots: camera motion reveals new evidence and character acting tells us who is considering it. Current opening has smooth legible motion (no obvious snap) but lacks the second character's readable role and a changed focal subject through the long middle hold.
