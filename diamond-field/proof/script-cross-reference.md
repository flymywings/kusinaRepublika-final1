## What the references demonstrate

The horse-race story puts the counterintuitive bet and personal stakes in its opening, then increases commitment before explaining the information advantage. The railroad story repeatedly changes the consequence of the same rail gaps. The oil story carries a concrete prop through danger, explanation, and escape.

Other useful comparisons: the lottery-clerk story reveals the fraud immediately and builds interest around its exposure; the furniture-house story ties repeated behavior to a financial consequence; the priest/knight story uses the same paid document to set up its reversal; the slot-machine story opens the mechanism so a verbal claim becomes understandable; the painkiller story uses a familiar analogy to explain an abstract process.

These are structural observations, not factual endorsements or permission to fabricate dialogue in a historical script. Catchphrases, ridicule, jokes, and analogies are useful only when the story needs them. The 20 references include different genres; a single compulsory formula would overstate their similarity.
