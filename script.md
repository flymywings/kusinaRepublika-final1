# When the shore gets wider

9:16 vertical · About 30 seconds · One calm narrator

## Narration

This beach can get wider just before a tsunami. To see why, look beneath the water. When an earthquake suddenly shifts the seabed, it can send waves across the sea. If the low part of a wave reaches shore first, the water pulls back and leaves sand exposed. That sudden retreat is a warning. If the sea suddenly pulls back like this, head straight to high ground. Don't wait to see the wave.

## Shot list

1. **This beach can get wider just before a tsunami.** Open on an illustrated Philippine beach. Drift toward the waterline as it recedes and exposes more sand.
2. **To see why, look beneath the water.** Follow the waterline seaward and tilt down into a side-on view of the seabed.
3. **When an earthquake suddenly shifts the seabed, it can send waves across the sea.** Glide past the fault as one slab lifts. Follow soft-blue ripples spreading across the surface.
4. **If the low part of a wave reaches shore first, the water pulls back and leaves sand exposed.** Track the dip back toward shore. Hold on the newly exposed sand as the waterline retreats.
5. **That sudden retreat is a warning.** Rise just enough to show the gap between the original and current waterlines.
6. **If the sea suddenly pulls back like this, head straight to high ground.** Pull inland and uphill, following a soft-blue route from the beach to the hill.
7. **Don't wait to see the wave.** Keep pulling back as the walker heads uphill, with the beach still visible below.

## Style line

Black-and-white scientific illustration with fine ink lines and soft graphite shading, like an old textbook. Each picture contains one isolated object. The animation adds all labels, numbers, and text and uses soft blue only to show light and movement.
