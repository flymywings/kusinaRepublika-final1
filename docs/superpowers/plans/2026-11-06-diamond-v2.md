## Review focus

1. The opening action and interested observer must read in two seconds; a moving clip through the complete sentence must sustain the question.
2. The field-sale premise must not look like ordinary jewelry trading.
3. Inspection, approval, and payment must form understandable cause and effect rather than disconnected shots.
4. The disturbed-versus-deeper-soil comparison must explain the clue before the London flashback.
5. Fixed prop identity, hand contact, planted feet, scene geography, captions, and voice clarity must survive merging and camera changes.

## Ownership and staging

Four subagent roles are used in stages, with at most three subagents active alongside the lead:

- Reference researcher: relevant actual sequences and source citations, including why the camera/action attracts attention. Read-only production access.
- World/props builder: World.tsx, props.tsx, paint.ts in a separate same-depth copy from the frozen V2 baseline.
- Cast/acting builder: Cast.tsx, draw.ts, acting.ts in another same-depth copy from that same baseline.
- Independent reviewer: moving previews and integrated footage, with no production-file ownership. For comprehension checks, first receive only neutral clips, not the intended explanation or script.

The lead owns contract.ts, story.ts, index.tsx, captions.ts, alignment, soundtrack, render.mjs, integration, and final delivery. Builders are not alone in the codebase: they must not revert others' work. Shared contract or timing changes return to the lead. Merge only owned files after the owner's preview passes, then review the integrated result again.
